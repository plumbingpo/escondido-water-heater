Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in San Marcos

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Typical Water Heater Issues in San Marcos

Around San Marcos, water heater trouble usually come from a handful of causes. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be part of the picture: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, and that's why regular descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A standard tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been used up, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Well water may bring sediment, iron, or sulfur into the tank, and a sulfur smell in the hot water often means a reaction with the anode rod that a different rod can fix. Older installations often need code updates when a heater is replaced: a thermal expansion tank where a check valve or pressure regulator creates a closed system, a properly routed T&P discharge line, drain pans where a leak could damage finished space, and earthquake straps in areas that require them. All of these can be fixed, and most cost far less to address early. If something seems off, a quick diagnostic visit can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor.
