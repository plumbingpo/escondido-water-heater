Keeping Your San Diego Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.
